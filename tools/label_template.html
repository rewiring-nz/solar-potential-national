Ridge, hip, valley, level change

Seen from above, which is how you’ll be looking at them.
Ridge
The line along the top where two slopes meet and rise to meet each other. It is the highest line on that part of the roof, and usually the longest. Rain runs away from it on both sides.
Hip
A sloping line running down to a corner, where two slopes meet on an outside angle. Think of the corner of a tent seen from above — the roof folds away from you. A ridge is flat along the top; a hip runs downhill from the end of a ridge to the eave.
Valley
The opposite of a hip: two slopes meeting on an inside angle, so rain collects and runs down it. You get one wherever two roof sections join, most often on an L or T shaped house.
Level change
Not a fold at all — a step where one part of the roof is simply higher than the next, with a little wall between them. Common on stepped houses down a slope, and around parapets on commercial roofs. If the two surfaces do not meet at an angle but at a height jump, it is this.
The quick test. Does the line sit at the top with roof falling away both sides? Ridge. Does it run downhill to a corner, with roof falling away both sides? Hip. Does it run downhill with roof rising away both sides, so water would sit in it? Valley. Is one side plainly higher than the other with a step between? Level change.

If you genuinely cannot tell, leave it out rather than guess — a wrong label is worse than a missing one.
